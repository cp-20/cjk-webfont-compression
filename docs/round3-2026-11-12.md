# Round 3 (2026-11-12): the stale tables are settled, the combined study goes CJK-wide, variable fonts get an honest answer, and the demo renders live

*A plain-language progress report. Everything numeric below was measured on real fonts in this
repository's environment; the JSON evidence is in `measure/out/`.*

## What this round did, in one paragraph

Round 2 left three debts: two published full-font tables that disagreed with each other (and
with re-measurement), a combined delivery×GLC study that only covered Japanese, and a demo
whose ε slider swapped pre-rendered pictures — pictures that, it turned out, were **upside
down**. This round pays all three debts and answers the variable-font question. The stale
tables are not just re-measured but *explained*: a forensic re-run proves they were produced
by an older quantiser and kept alive by a file-exists cache. The combined study now covers
Chinese and Korean with the same harness, and the "layers multiply" finding holds in all
three scripts. Variable fonts get a measured, mostly negative answer: quantising a variable
font directly is structurally sound but buys little (−8.6 % on the all-weights file) and its
worst-case outline error grows ~35× away from the default weight, so the shipped tool keeps
instancing as the recommended flow and offers direct quantisation only as a labelled
experiment. And the demo now loads the actual quantised fonts into the browser and renders
your text with them, live.

## 1. The stale tables: verdict and corrections

Round 2 established that the M PLUS 1p quantised rows of `glc_fullsweep.json` and the whole
quantised side of `glc_fullfont.json` did not reproduce, and suspected stale cached
intermediates (`prep_s = 0.0`). Round 3's `measure/remeasure_fullfont.py` re-measured both
tables in a clean room (fresh work directory, no cache reuse) and added a forensic arm: the
same fonts run through the current pipeline with BBox preservation switched **off**
(`keep_bbox=False`) — i.e. the quantiser as it behaved before the BBox fix.

**Verdict: the stale values are the pre-BBox-fix quantiser's output, to within WOFF2 encode
wobble.** The forensic arm reproduces the published `glc_fullfont` Noto rows almost exactly
(grid 4: 1,921,148 vs published 1,921,116 B; grid 8: 1,667,496 vs 1,667,400 B; grid 16:
1,430,100 vs 1,429,436 B — all within 664 B), while the current pipeline lands on the
full-sweep values. The mechanism is the one suspected: the sweep scripts reused existing
intermediate TTFs via an exists-check, so rows measured before the BBox fix survived,
unmeasured, into the published JSON (`prep_s = 0.0`).

Corrected values (regenerated into both JSONs; the two tables now agree by construction):

| Font / grid | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M PLUS 1p, was | 704,924 | 643,020 | 579,796 | 514,728 | 449,352 |
| M PLUS 1p, corrected | **711,776** | **666,212** | **614,540** | **559,844** | **505,212** |
| Noto (fullfont table), was | — | 1,921,116 | 1,667,400 | 1,429,436 | — |
| Noto (fullfont table), corrected | — | **1,980,272** | **1,755,444** | **1,547,056** | — |

The Noto full-sweep rows were also regenerated and moved by ≤0.08 % (encode/instancing
wobble only), so the headline — 2,302,848 → 1,755,444 B at grid 8, −23.8 % — stands exactly
as published. The M PLUS 1p headline weakens honestly: at grid 8 the saving is −12.2 %
(699,792 → 614,540 B), not the −17.1 % the stale row implied.

What was corrected, and how:

- `measure/out/glc_fullfont.json` and `measure/out/glc_fullsweep.json` regenerated with
  always-fresh intermediates; `measure/glc_fullfont.py` / `glc_fullsweep.py` no longer reuse
  intermediates (the exists-check cache is removed — it is what let stale rows persist).
- `report/glc.html` (Appendix C): the quoted English abstract still contains the old numbers
  — it is a translation of record, so instead of silently rewriting it, a bilingual
  correction note now precedes it, giving the corrected values (Noto 1.980 / 1.755 / 1.547 MB;
  M PLUS grid 8: 579,796 → 614,540 B) and pointing here. The one remaining in-text use of
  1.667 MB is the deliberate with/without-BBox comparison, which the forensic arm confirms.
- Evidence: `measure/out/glc_remeasure.json` (fresh rows for both fonts and grids, plus the
  forensic arm).

## 2. The combined study, now in three scripts

`measure/combined_study_zhko.py` runs the round-2 harness unchanged on the corpus's Chinese
and Korean pages (character sets from `measure/web/corpus_stats.json`; Noto Sans SC / KR
static 400 instances). Coverage is thinner than Japanese and is disclosed in the JSONs:
Chinese has 7 usable pages; Korean only 4 — Naver and Daum have no recorded character set or
Google Fonts estimate and are excluded, by name, in `combined_study_ko.json`.

Median per-page payload (bytes):

| Arm | 日本語 (31 pages) | 中文 (7 pages) | 한국어 (4 pages) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full font, stock WOFF2 (ref) | 2,303,000 | 4,192,748 | 2,073,072 |
| Google Fonts slicing (audit) | 521,392 (509.2 KiB) | 732,840 | 390,964 |
| Exact subset, stock | 104,912 (102.5 KiB) | 147,976 | 72,020 |
| Exact subset + GLC g=8 | 90,716 (88.6 KiB) | 120,824 | 60,964 |
| Base-500 + residual, cold | 140,108 (136.8 KiB) | 155,204 | 75,232 |
| Base-500 + residual, warm | 23,564 (23.0 KiB) | 76,800 | 26,840 |
| Base-500 + residual + GLC, cold | 120,676 (117.8 KiB) | 127,728 | 64,500 |
| Base-500 + residual + GLC, warm | **19,800 (19.3 KiB)** | **62,052** | **22,536** |

(Japanese medians were published in KiB in round 2; the byte values are the same medians.
Full numbers: `measure/out/combined_study{,_zh,_ko}.json`.)

Findings:

1. **The layers multiply in all three scripts.** GLC's median gain on exact subsets at grid 8
   is 15.2 % (ja), 18.4 % (zh), 15.6 % (ko); on the shared base slice, 13.4 % / 16.2 % /
   13.3 %. The best combined arm remains base+GLC warm in every language: −96.2 % (ja),
   −91.5 % (zh), −94.2 % (ko) against the Google Fonts median.
2. **The "gain grows with subset size" shape repeats.** Anchor-subset gains at grid 8 grow
   monotonically in Chinese (13.5 % at 100 chars → 20.4 % at 2,000) and Korean (9.3 % at 100 →
   16.9 % at 1,000), as in Japanese (12.5 % at 300 → 19.8 % at 3,000). Same mechanism: fixed
   per-file overhead that quantisation cannot shrink.
3. **No robust cross-script ranking.** At the smallest anchors Korean starts lower (9.3 % at
   100 chars vs ja 14.1 %, zh 13.5 %) but converges and even passes Japanese by 1,000 chars
   (16.9 % vs 15.5 %); the Japanese anchors themselves are non-monotonic below 500 chars.
   With 4–7 pages per language these are corpus-level wobbles, not script properties — the
   robust claims are findings 1 and 2, which hold in all three.
4. **Chinese pages are heavier at every layer** — the full SC font is 4.19 MB and its Google
   Fonts median is 732,840 B — so the absolute win from combining layers is largest there:
   the warm base+GLC arm moves ~671 KB per page versus the GF baseline.

## 3. Variable fonts: measured, and mostly a negative result

`tools/glc.py` used to refuse an uninstanced variable font outright. Round 3 measured both
candidate shapes on Noto Sans JP variable at grid 8 (`measure/variable_glc.py`,
`measure/out/variable_glc.json`):

- **Arm A — instance, then GLC** (the existing flow): one static GLC file per used weight.
- **Arm B — quantise the variable font directly**: round the default outlines once, keep
  `gvar`, ship a single file for all weights. A VF-safe prototype quantiser
  (`glyphcodec.process_variable_font`: simple glyphs only, composites and `gvar` untouched,
  hinting stripped without expanding composites) makes this structurally possible: at every
  probed weight (100–900) the instanced outlines have **zero point-count mismatches** — the
  glyf↔gvar index sync survives.

The catch is geometric, and it is decisive. `gvar`'s IUP interpolation *amplifies* the
quantisation perturbation away from the default weight: mean outline error stays ≈2.6 units
everywhere, but the **maximum point error grows from 4 units at wght=100 to 138 units at
wght=900** (grid 8; the ε budget at the default weight is 4 units). A grid-2 spot check
(`measure/variable_glc_spotcheck.py`) shows the amplification is not proportional to the
perturbation: worst case at wght=900 is still 88 units — and at grid 2 the quantised file is
within 0.1 % of stock size, so the fine-grid route buys nothing at all. Even at grid 8, the
single all-weights file saves only **−8.6 %** — far below the −23.8 % the same grid achieves
on a static instance — because `gvar` delta data dominates the file and does not quantise.

**Product decision, shipped in `tools/glc.py`:** `--instance` per weight remains the
recommended flow (its error guarantee is intact: error ≤ ε at the weight you ship). The new
`--quantize-variable` is an explicitly opt-in experiment that prints the measured caveat
(error amplification away from the default weight; expect single-digit savings). Subsetting
a variable font, and CFF2 variable fonts, are still refused. This is recorded as a negative
result: direct VF quantisation works, and is not worth it with this codec design; a
gvar-aware quantiser that rounds deltas jointly with defaults would be a different project.

## 4. The demo: live fonts instead of pictures, and an upside-down bug buried

**The bug.** Round 2's demo images were rendered by `measure/glc_demo.py`, whose canvas
mapping was `y = px + top - row`. FreeType bitmap row 0 is the glyph's *top* row and
`bitmap_top` is measured upward from the baseline, so the correct mapping is
`y = px - top + row`; the shipped formula mirrored every glyph vertically. Naoki spotted it
("なんか逆さになってる") — the round-2 review had not. Fixed in `measure/glc_demo.py` and
`measure/glc_render.py`.

**Did it corrupt the published metrics? No — verified, not assumed.**
`measure/verify_render_orientation.py` renders the same text with both formulas and shows
(1) the buggy canvas is the *exact* vertical mirror of the fixed one (column ink profiles
identical, row profile exactly reversed), and (2) the fidelity metrics are numerically
identical under both renderers (changed pixels 4,246 of 29,184 = 14.549 %, max difference 45,
in both). Mirror-vs-mirror comparisons cancel the flip, so every published PSNR / changed-
pixel number stands. Only the pictures were wrong.

**The rebuild** (`report/build_demo.py` → `report/demo.html`; assets by
`measure/build_demo_assets.py`):

- The ε slider no longer swaps PNGs. Six real subset WOFF2 files
  (`report/assets/demo_font_g{0,2,4,8,16,32}.woff2`, Noto Sans JP subset to a 3,395-character
  demo charset — every character in the 31-page Japanese corpus plus kana/ASCII/punctuation
  blocks) are loaded on demand through the FontFace API, and the preview text is re-rendered
  by the browser with the actual quantised font at two sizes (52 px and 20 px).
- The preview text is editable. Characters outside the demo charset are marked in red and
  drawn with a fallback font, with a live count — no silent tofu.
- Readouts are live: the shipped subset file's actual size and Δ vs g=0, plus fidelity
  (PSNR / changed pixels / max difference at 24 px) measured *on the shipped assets
  themselves* with the fixed renderer. Grid 8: 446,768 B (−19.3 % vs grid 0 on this subset),
  35.30 dB, 13.51 % pixels changed.
- Section 3 is now an interactive layer explorer: animated bars for all eight delivery×GLC
  arms with a 日本語 / 中文 / 한국어 switcher (medians from the three combined studies),
  replacing the static Japanese-only table.
- The section-1 payload estimator is unchanged (Japanese anchors, labelled estimates).
- The old upside-down PNGs are removed from `report/assets/`; nothing references them.

**Verification.** All six WOFF2 assets parse as WOFF2 and their cmaps cover 100 % of the
demo charset (4,441 glyphs each, 0 missing). The page's JavaScript was exercised end-to-end
in jsdom with a stubbed FontFace (23 assertions: initial g=8 load and readouts, slider moves
to g=32/g=0 including the ∞-PSNR label, out-of-subset marking, language switching, bar
animation, estimator updates — all pass). A real-Chromium run was attempted but this
sandbox's browser build refuses loopback navigation (`ERR_BLOCKED_BY_LOCAL_NETWORK_ACCESS_CHECKS`),
so the jsdom run plus asset validation is the evidence; the FontFace calls themselves are
the standard API used identically in production browsers.

## 5. What is next

- **A gvar-aware quantiser design study** (not code yet): round deltas jointly with defaults
  so IUP cannot amplify error — the only route that could make direct VF quantisation
  competitive; §3's numbers are the bar it must clear.
- **Korean corpus depth**: 4 pages is thin. Extending `corpus_stats.json` coverage for ko
  (and zh) would firm up §2's cross-script claims.
- **`report/index.html` still cannot be regenerated from scratch** (it reads the
  non-redistributed `measure/web/corpus.json`; sections silently drop without it). Making
  the delivery report's generator degrade loudly, or restructuring it onto
  `corpus_stats.json`, remains open from round 2.
- **Per-weight variable-font bundles**: Arm A numbers (§3) quantify what a site pays per
  added weight; a "which weights are worth instancing" analysis on real pages is the
  practical follow-up.
- The demo's live rendering could extend to a **zh/ko preview** (assets for SC/KR subsets)
  if the demo sees real use.
